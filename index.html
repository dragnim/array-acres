APL is a programming language built around arrays: lists, tables and grids of values. Unlike many languages, APL lets you work with entire arrays at once, often expressing complex operations in just a few symbols. APL grew out of a mathematical notation developed by Kenneth E. Iverson, described in his 1962 book A Programming Language, which gave APL its name. It became a working programming language at IBM in the 1960s and is still used today, particularly in finance and insurance.

Whole arrays at once

⍵=Ripe checks every plot on the field at once, and gives back a whole field of 1s and 0s: 1 for ripe, 0 for everything else.

Why it suits farming

A field is a grid, and grids are exactly what APL is built for. Every harvest decision is a question about the whole field: which plants are ripe, which are rotten, where a bumper crop could form. In APL each question is a short expression, and combining them takes a single symbol, like ∨ for “or”. That’s why a whole farming strategy can fit on one line.

Why the symbols?

Each symbol is a built-in function, just like + or ×. They look unfamiliar at first, but once you learn them, they let you express complex ideas concisely. Iverson called this “notation as a tool of thought”, the title of his Turing Award lecture.

Find out more on the APL Wiki, or try more APL in your browser at tryapl.org.
